Privacy policy

By Matt Kerr·Published 6 August 2026·Updated 10 November 2026

Check Engine is desktop software that runs on your Mac or Windows PC. This policy covers both the application and this website.

Check Engine for Windows is the same application and makes the same requests. Where this policy says Mac, read the Windows PC you run it on.

The application

Nothing about the sites you audit reaches us, Kerr & Company: the crawl and the report stay on your computer. The requests that do leave it are listed below with who receives each one, and the ones that come to us are anonymous app counts, requests for the public update, price and knowledge files on this website and, on a copy with no licence, a question to an offer server we run. What our payment provider receives on our behalf is your licence key and a name for each Mac you activate it on. There is no account or crash reporting. The licence key is checked with our payment provider when you activate it and about once a day after that.

An earlier version of this policy said the only network requests the app makes are to the website you ask it to audit, and the next one named three kinds of destination. Neither was accurate, and a privacy policy is the last document that should be approximately true. This is every request the shipped app makes, read from its code, starting with the ones it makes unless you switch them off.

What it does by default

  • The licence check, to Dodo Payments. Dodo handles our checkout and issues the licence keys. When you activate Check Engine, the app sends Dodo your licence key and a name for that activation: Check Engine @ followed by the computer's hostname: on a Mac, the network name macOS usually builds from the computer's name; on Windows, the computer name. After that it sends the key with that activation's id about once a day, when the app opens or an audit starts, and again if you deactivate. None of these requests carries anything about your audits, and no switch turns them off.
  • The update check, to this website. When the app opens, and every thirty minutes while it stays open, it asks docketseo.app/updater.json whether a newer version exists; Check for Updates… in the Check Engine menu asks on demand. It sends nothing but the request for that file. If you accept an update, the new build is downloaded from this site's releases on GitHub, and nothing installs without you saying yes. No switch turns the check off.
  • The offer, to this website and to our offer server. Only on a copy with no licence key, and only while its activation screen is open, the app asks docketseo.app/offer.json for the current price and any founding offer, and asks kerr-lead-agent.kerrco.workers.dev, a server we run, whether the founding offer is still open, sending only this site’s name. That is how it avoids showing an offer that has ended. Nothing else is sent. With “Keep it private” ticked in the crawl settings it asks neither, and shows the list price alone. Buy opens our buy page in your browser.
  • Google PageSpeed Insights, for Core Web Vitals. The addresses of up to five of your shallowest pages, homepage first, each sent to Google's PageSpeed Insights API, which then measures that page. It works without an API key, and the desktop app never sends one.
  • DNS, for email deliverability. The mail domains of the addresses your pages publish, leaving out free providers such as Gmail. Check Engine looks up their mail-server (MX) and address records, asking your computer's own DNS servers first and Cloudflare's and Google's public resolvers if those do not answer. Where a domain has no MX record, it also opens a connection to port 25 on that domain's server to see whether anything is listening, and closes it without sending a message.
  • Google's autocomplete, for topic suggestions. The topic you type in “What is this site about?” or, if you leave it empty, up to three words that recur in the titles, headings and descriptions of at least a quarter of your pages, leaving out the site's own name unless nothing else recurs. Each is sent to Google's public autocomplete on its own and with question and buying phrases added, such as “how to” and “cost”. If you type nothing and no word recurs, nothing is sent.
  • This website, for the knowledge file. The knowledge refresh fetches docketseo.app/data/knowledge.json: the current AI crawler list, Core Web Vitals thresholds and ranking notes. It is a plain request for one public file. Its user-agent says Check Engine, and it carries nothing about you or what you are auditing.
  • The site you asked it to audit. The crawl itself, from your machine, naming itself as Check Engine, and the edge-access checks, which request your homepage again as Check Engine and once as each of Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User and Meta-WebIndexer, naming Check Engine in each, to see what your server tells them.
  • Other sites your pages point to. Check Engine checks that a sample of your links to other sites still work, and that the share images your pages name load, with a request to each address and a second if the first fails. It fetches the stylesheets your pages link, including ones served from other hosts. And it renders a small sample of your pages in WebKit on a Mac, or Microsoft’s WebView2 (Chromium) on Windows, which runs their scripts the way a browser would, so whatever those pages load, such as analytics or advertising pixels, is requested too.

What turns each off

The offline switch turns off five of these together: PageSpeed Insights, the deliverability lookups, topic suggestions, the knowledge refresh and the edge-access checks. In the app it is the box labelled “Keep it private: only contact this site”, and on the command line it is --offline. It also keeps stylesheet fetches to the site you audit. It does not turn off the licence check, the update check, the link check or rendering. Unticking “Also check that links to other sites still work” in the app, or --no-link-check, turns off the link check, and rendering can be turned off only on the command line, with --render 0. There is no separate switch for each of the five: the offline switch is the one control. These runs ignore it, and always make the five checks and render pages as usual: re-audits of the sites you save, whether on their schedule, which runs only while Check Engine is open, or from “Check now”; checkengine diff; and checkengine logs --url.

What it does only when you ask

  • checkengine backlinks and checkengine attack read Common Crawl's public link graph from commoncrawl.org. Each download reads a public file from its beginning rather than asking for your domain, so the request does not name the domain you are asking about. --referring streams larger files the same way, and --no-authority skips it in checkengine attack.
  • checkengine attack --demand sends the phrase you give, or one Check Engine reads off the site, to Google's autocomplete in the same way as topic suggestions.
  • checkengine logs --verify looks up each address in your log file that claims to be Googlebot in DNS, to confirm it is Google's.
  • --psi-key on the command line sends your own PageSpeed Insights key to Google with those requests.
  • An AI assistant you connect with checkengine mcp runs audits that make the same requests as any other, and it can ask for offline mode.

Audit results, your saved-site list and any crash reports are stored on your machine in ~/.docket/ as plain JSON files. They are never transmitted. Deleting that folder removes them permanently.

What the app tells us, anonymously

The app sends one count at each of these three moments:

  • first_opened: the first time the app is opened.
  • first_audit_finished: the first time an audit finishes.
  • free_limit: the first time the free version reaches its limit and shows the unlock.

Each is sent once per copy and per event. Each sends only the event name, the app version and the kind of computer: Mac, Windows or Linux. It is a single HTTPS GET, like a page request, to mcp.docketseo.app/app-event, with a User-Agent header, no cookie and no body.

These counts never send any web address or the site you audit, results or reports, your licence key, any ID or name, or anything from your files. Like any page request, the receiving server sees your internet address for the moment it answers. We store only a daily tally per event, version and system, never who. We do not store the internet address or User-Agent with the counts.

Nothing is sent for these counts by source builds or by the engine's offline mode, and an audit run with “Keep it private: only contact this site” switched on sends no count when it finishes. The first count, first_opened, goes out the first time the app starts, before there is a chance to choose. To turn all three counts off, set the environment variable DOCKET_NO_COUNTS=1, or create an empty file named no-counts in the .docket folder in your home folder, before you first open the app.

Refund and cancellation policy

By Matt Kerr·Published 11 August 2026·Updated 9 November 2026

Thirty days from the date of purchase, no conditions and no questions asked, on any paid copy of Check Engine. What follows is how to ask, what happens to the copy on your computer afterwards, and why the window is thirty days rather than a number picked because it sounded generous.

The policy

Ask within 30 days of the date on your receipt and the full purchase price goes back to the payment method it came from, along with any tax charged on top of it. Check Engine is sold as one item at one price, so there is no partial refund to calculate and none is offered.

You do not have to have stopped using it, give a reason, or send evidence of anything.

Why thirty days

Three reasons, and each is a fact about this product rather than a convention:

  • The free version shows how much is wrong, and the worst of it. It gives the score, how many problems each area has and the top problem in full with its fix, so the rest of the findings, the fix plan and the markup come after the money does. Thirty days with no conditions is what makes that fair: run it on your own sites and your clients', and if it is not worth the price to you, ask for it back.
  • Monitoring needs weeks to say anything. The default re-audit cadence is weekly, and the thing being sold (what changed on your site since last time) is empty on the first run and thin on the second. Thirty days is four of them. A fourteen-day window would end before the feature had demonstrated itself, which would make it a policy that quietly excluded the reason some people bought.
  • It can be honoured by one person. Issuing a refund is one action in the dashboard at Dodo Payments. Nothing has to be reclaimed, deactivated, closed or chased, so the length of the window costs nothing but the money.

How to ask

Refund requests go to support@docketseo.app, or as a reply to the receipt Dodo Payments sends you. Either arrives.

Please do not open a GitHub issue about a refund. The tracker is the contact channel for everything else here and it is deliberately public, which is right for a wrong finding and wrong for a payment: a refund request carries your name, an order reference and sometimes the reason you are unhappy, and none of the three belong on a page anyone can read.

What happens to your copy

It goes back to the free version. Taking a refund revokes the licence key that came with your purchase, and Check Engine re-checks that key about once a day, so within roughly a day of the refund being issued, the copy on your computer shows only the score, how many problems each area has and the top problem. A revoked key is refused immediately and is not given the offline grace period that a merely-unreachable licence server would allow.

This changed when Check Engine became a licensed product in August 2026. Before that it had no licence server at all and a refunded copy kept working indefinitely; that is no longer true, and it would be worse to leave the old promise standing than to say so plainly. Your audit history is untouched either way: see below.

What is asked is that you delete the application and stop using it. What that rests on is that you will. The licence granted by the terms of use ends the day the refund is issued; the enforcement of it is a sentence rather than a switch, and pretending otherwise would describe software that was not built.

Your audit history is yours and stays yours. It lives on your machine in ~/.docket/ as plain JSON: sites.json for the watchlist and history/ for the snapshots. Nothing there was ever uploaded, so nothing there is deleted from our side, because there is no our side. Removing that folder removes it.

Conditions that are deliberately absent

None of these will be asked, because none of them could be checked. Check Engine has no account. Its anonymous counts identify no copy and contain no audit result. The licence key ties a payment to the Macs it is activated on and to nothing else, so the seller has no way to know:

  • how many sites you audited;
  • how many PDF or CSV reports you exported, or who you sent them to;
  • whether you are asking for the first time or the fourth.

A refund policy conditioned on facts the seller cannot verify is one applied by mood, and that is worse than having no condition at all. So there are none, and this paragraph exists so that the absence reads as a decision rather than an oversight.

One correction to make in the same breath, because a page claiming perfect ignorance would be exactly the sort of claim this tool exists to check. Check Engine makes two requests of its own accord. It checks the licence key with our payment provider when you activate it and about once a day after that, which is how a refund reaches the copy on your computer. And when it opens, and every thirty minutes while it stays open, it asks docketseo.app/updater.json whether a newer build exists, and stays silent if there is none or there is no network. That file is static and served by GitHub Pages. The log behind it records an address and a time, the way any page fetch does, and it carries no identifier that could be matched against an order, but it is named here rather than left out.

When the money arrives

The refund is issued to the original payment method, because that is what card rules require and it is also the only route that reliably works. How long it takes to appear on your statement sits between Dodo Payments and your bank, and neither answers to us. No number of days is promised here for the same reason no response time is promised on the contact page: an invented figure is worse than an admitted unknown.

Chargebacks

A chargeback and a refund move the same money in the same direction. Ask first: it is faster for you, and a disputed charge is answered with the same refund plus a copy of this page, several weeks later, after a fee. If a refund has been asked for and not answered, raise the chargeback; that is what the mechanism is for.

If you are buying from outside the United States

Consumer law where you live may give you rights this policy cannot shorten, and nothing on this page attempts to. In the UK and across the EU a distance sale of digital content carries a 14-day right to cancel, and a seller is allowed to ask you to give it up in exchange for an immediate download. Check Engine does not ask you to give it up. The 30 days above are longer and carry fewer conditions, so the waiver would buy the buyer nothing and cost the seller a paragraph of small print.
